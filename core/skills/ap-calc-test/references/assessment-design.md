# Assessment design and difficulty calibration

These are learner-specific defaults established on 2026-10-06, not a universal AP blueprint. Use the requested scope and actual teacher materials to decide which apply.

## Blueprint before writing

Privately map each part to a source concept, representation, required decision, expected work/time, marks, prerequisite skills, and a plausible error. Report the actual FRQ share of marks/time in the handoff. Use short MCQs for diagnostic breadth and multipart FRQs for connected reasoning. Add manageable entry parts and substantive later parts without revealing later answers. Make downstream parts independently accessible or allow explicit error-follow-through credit when appropriate.

A chapter mock should test recall, execution, selecting a method, translating representations, and explaining a conclusion. Cover requested knowledge points through this map, not by repeating one template under different numbers. Identify gaps in available scope evidence instead of claiming complete coverage blindly.

## Calibration from the learner's teacher test

- **Motion:** one position function supports displacement and average velocity over a specified interval, instantaneous velocity/acceleration at an instant, and average acceleration. Include nonzero starting times and sometimes nonzero initial velocity so shortcuts such as velocity divided by time fail. Specify units, domain and every averaging interval. Total distance, speed changes and integration only belong when within the taught scope.
- **Mixed representations:** a graph for one function, a table of another function and its derivative, and a piecewise formula for a third. Ask for product/quotient/composition derivatives and estimates from table values. Supply every necessary coordinate and derivative lookup, ensure denominators are nonzero where required, and distinguish graph height from slope. Show open/closed endpoints and exact segment/arc information; do not require guessing from a sketch.
- **Differentiability:** distinguish function value, continuity, limit existence and derivative existence explicitly. Require one-sided evidence for a join, corner, cusp or vertical tangent. State the function's domain and check continuity before classifying a finite point. Opposite infinite slopes support a vertical cusp; matching infinite slopes support a vertical tangent in appropriate examples. Check the defining difference quotients when proving derivative existence/nonexistence rather than relying only on a failed derivative formula.
- **Limit process:** include suitable examples where combining fractions, factoring or a conjugate is essential. Confirm cancellation is valid for the approaching variable away from its limiting value. Do not give away the needed algebraic trick in the heading.
- **Transfer:** change more than numbers. Vary which quantity is given, where it is evaluated, how information is represented, and the context. Stay within the requested unit even when public full-year FRQs contain later topics.

## Private rubric and grading

Solve every question independently of the intended key. Check numerical values, signs, units, domains and alternative equivalent forms. Award transparent method/setup/result/justification credit as appropriate; do not penalize unsimplified correct expressions unless the task requires a form. Specify consequences of arithmetic errors and allow consistent follow-through when justified. Record ambiguous or insufficient student work as such rather than inventing reasoning. Separate independent performance from work corrected after hints. A short custom chapter set does not establish an official AP score.
