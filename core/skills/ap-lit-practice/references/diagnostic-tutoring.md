# Diagnostic tutoring

Separate vocabulary (poem and question), stem-target parsing, literal comprehension, syntax, referents, metaphor roles, purpose/consequence, speaker feeling versus authorial tone, unsupported inference, and test execution.

A wrong letter may arise from several causes. Diagnose provisionally until the learner explains. Scores describe this attempt, not intelligence or permanent ability.

Useful distinctions:
- Not knowing "analogous" can block an otherwise understood comparison.
- Mistaking "lucid" for unconsciousness is vocabulary, not poor character analysis.
- A salt/grief metaphor can be valid but irrelevant to a question asking what "numbers" refers to.
- A nervous character can be presented comically.
These are examples, not fixed assumptions about future learners.

## Confusion

When the learner says "I don't understand," stop the multi-question explanation.
1. Give the relevant actual sentence or surrounding lines.
2. Paraphrase the literal situation simply.
3. Use one restrained analogy if useful.
4. Ask one focused comprehension question.
5. Connect their answer back to the original wording/choice.
6. Later check transfer on an unfamiliar item.

Don't let an analogy invent plot evidence. A private-journal analogy might clarify emotional containment, but doesn't establish a leaked secret or mocking audience. If the learner inherits an unsupported detail from your analogy, acknowledge and repair it.

Preserve the valid insight and correct the narrow mismatch. If a question is challenged as unfair, explain its exact target and concede real ambiguity instead of defending the key by authority.

Avoid endless leading binary questions. As understanding improves, ask for independent reasoning. "Oh" or agreement alone is not mastery.

## Feedback

Give the score and uncertainty, identify misses, and explain the most useful patterns. Explain every miss when requested, then slow down if needed.

For a substantive miss:
- Identify their reasoning when known.
- Quote a short decisive phrase.
- Explain why the keyed relationship fits.
- Identify what the competing choice imports, omits, or confuses.

Don't invent motives for characters or learners. Define literary terms plainly; don't replace the poem with a generic moral.

Use confidence and unknown-word notes:
- Correct/low confidence: ask for evidence before assuming a guess.
- Wrong/high confidence: check misconceptions or stem misreading.
- Wrong/unknown word: define it, then recheck interpretation without calling the prompted response independent success.

Keep difficulty internal unless asked. It is an estimate, not a reason to excuse a defective item or inflate a grade. Adapt the next unseen set to confirmed needs and verify retention.
