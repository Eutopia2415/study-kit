# Building question sets the way the AP Business with Personal Finance exam does

Observed from the Course and Exam Description (Effective Fall 2026, exam information pages 237–260). First exam May 2027, fully digital in Bluebook, four-function calculator allowed. The tutor may build mimic sets; a real CED sample or AP Classroom item beats a mimic when one fits.

MC covers Units 1–4: Unit 1 20–30%, Unit 2 20–30%, Unit 3 (both parts) 25–35%, Unit 4 15–20%, with 12–15 personal-finance items throughout. Skills on the MC section: concept application 45–55%, entrepreneurship 5–15%, decision making 25–35%, communication 5–15%. About 70 seconds per item.

## How the items are built (what to copy)

- **Set-based.** A scenario of a paragraph plus one or two figures (a survey, a cost table, a chart), then 4 questions on it. The CED samples: Edi's Electronics Shop (a SWOT informed by a customer survey, two figures), Bridger Skate Company (a startup's price against rivals' costs). Questions on one set move from reading the data, to naming the concept at work, to the decision criterion the business would use.
- **Stems.** "Which of the following conclusions is supported by the data in the figure?", "best describes a market opportunity revealed by", "is most likely to reveal which of the following findings", "which of the following criteria would X be most likely to use, given its business goal", "best explains why X would".
- **Distractors are plausible concept confusions**, not nonsense: price gouging / collusion / penetration / cost-based pricing in one item; finance / operations / HR / sales as the department behind market research; a data-reading distractor that swaps the base of a percentage. In a mimic, each wrong choice is a named confusion.
- **FRQ task verbs:** compare, describe, explain (how = the relationship or process; why = the reasons), identify (no elaboration), pitch, recommend (a course of action with evidence). FRQ 4 wants decision criteria stated first, both alternatives compared on them, then a recommendation supported by the scenario's own numbers and a limitation.
- The case answers in class (Merck) are the same shape: the answer that scores uses the case's facts, not the textbook definition.

## Sources on disk

Real items come first. When the learner adds one of these to this folder, list it in RESOURCES.md and use it from there:
- The College Board Course and Exam Description (CED) for this course, free on AP Central: its sample questions come with keys and a skill table.
- Released free-response questions with scoring guidelines (AP Central, past years).
- AP Classroom results the learner exports or pastes.
- The teacher's own worksheets, reviews and tests, which outrank everything else as the model for this class.

## Building a set

1. Prefer real items: an unused CED sample set or an AP Classroom item.
2. Otherwise build a mimic with the `ap-business-practice` skill: one scenario with a figure, 4–6 four-choice questions that run data → concept → decision, each with the key, unit, skill tag and the confusion behind each distractor; for a case block, add one FRQ 4-style decision with two alternatives and a scoring note (criteria, comparison, recommendation, limitation).
3. Save to `practice/YYYY-MM-DD_<topic>.md`. The heading says **mimic, not released**.
4. After: score, every miss to its `SKILLS.md` row, one log line below. A written FRQ answer is graded on the chain fact → mechanism → consequence → recommendation → limitation.
