# Grading an essay (the tutor's procedure)

The bar is a 5 on the exam, which on one essay means 5/6: thesis (A) 1, evidence and commentary (B) 3 or 4, sophistication (C) usually 0. The full rubric and the scored samples are in `../AP_Lit_Q1_Calibration_Set.md`. Grade for control: most essays lose their points on control, not ideas.

## The pass, in order

0. **Inside a 70-minute block the budget is fixed:** 5 minutes of claim ladder on the prompt, 40 of essay with the stop time posted and called at minute 35, 10 of feedback, 10 of rewrite, 5 of close (retry the essay's hardest move, alone, no cues), and the close is protected whatever overruns. Before the clock, one check on the claim: which decision does the character make differently because of it, and who pays? No answer: the claim is the work, not the essay. Feedback is **one exchange, not a verdict**: name the capping sentence and what it does wrong, then the learner says back in their own words why it fails before touching the rewrite. If they can't, that is the gap, and the rewrite waits one more question. Everything else waits for the log.
1. **Read the whole essay once without marking.** Then the prompt again. Did it answer every part?
2. **Row A.** Quote the thesis sentence. Is it defensible (a reader could disagree) and does it answer the prompt's tension? A restatement of the prompt is 0.
3. **Row B.** Find the weakest commentary sentence and quote it. Name what it does: names a device without saying what the device does (that's a 2), says what it does but claims more than the quoted words carry (the 3 → 4 gap), or earns its claim from the words (4). Score the row by the pattern across the essay, not the best paragraph. The 2/3 line is whether a line of reasoning exists at all, not whether each paragraph adds a new claim: paragraphs that repeat one claim with different evidence can still be a 3; a new claim per paragraph is the 4 bar.
4. **Row C.** Default 0. Give 1 only for something a chief reader would: a tension held through the essay, an alternative reading answered, prose consistently vivid and controlled. Nationally about 4 in 100 essays get it.
5. **Nearest sample.** For Q1 use the calibration set; for Q2 and Q3 use the released scored samples from AP Central (save them under `released/` and list them in RESOURCES.md) (three per question per year, with reader commentary). Name the scored sample this essay most resembles and the one score higher, and say in one sentence what the higher one does that this one doesn't. The samples calibrate the scorer; they are not a template the reading has to resemble. A different interpretation with the same control scores the same.
6. **One rewrite, not a list.** Pick the one paragraph whose fix would move the score. The learner rewrites it (10 minutes). Grade the rewrite in one line.
7. **Say the number plainly.** "This is a 3, and here's the sentence that caps it." Not "a solid attempt".

## What to record

- `LOG.md`: one row per essay and one per rewrite, with the weakest sentence quoted and the fix for next time in ten words or fewer. The timed essay is independent evidence; the rewrite follows feedback, so it is assisted (`correct_with_help` in Graphiti, never a step toward shown).
- `../SKILLS.md`: the Writing row for the task (Q1/Q2/Q3) and the Claim creation, Voice or Sophistication rows the essay showed, with Last and Next.
- `../STATE.md`: the gap, if the same fix comes up twice.

## Common control problems, and the drill for each

| Problem | Drill in `DRILLS.md` |
|---|---|
| Claim bigger than the quoted words | Shrink the claim |
| Good idea in exploratory language, not a claim | Claim ladder; The tension sentence |
| Prompt not fully answered | Prompt parts, ticked |
| Device named, nothing said about what it does | Two-sentence commentary |
| "shows", "uses", "conveys" everywhere | Verb swap |
| Plot summary where an argument should be (Q3) | Introduce without summary |
| Register drifts to chat | Register pass |
