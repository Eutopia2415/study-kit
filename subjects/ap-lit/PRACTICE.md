# Building question sets the way the AP Literature exam does

Observed from the Course and Exam Description (exam information pages 131–176, with sample items, answer key and skill alignment) and released free-response sets. The tutor may build mimic sets; a real released passage set, CED sample or AP Classroom item beats a mimic when one fits and hasn't been used.

## The exam

| Section | Questions | Weight | Time |
|---|---|---|---|
| I Multiple choice | 55 | 45% | 60 min |
| II Free response: Q1 poetry, Q2 prose fiction, Q3 literary argument | 3 | 55% | 120 min |

MC: five passage sets of 8 to 13 questions, at least two prose fiction and at least two poetry, texts from many periods with more 20th-century and contemporary than earlier. Just over a minute per item. Skill weighting on MC: narrator or speaker 21–26%, character 16–20%, plot and structure 16–20%, word choice, imagery and symbol 10–13%, comparison 10–13%, literary argumentation 10–13%, setting 3–6%. Unit weighting: short fiction 42–49%, poetry 36–45%, longer fiction or drama 15–18%.

## How the items are built (what to copy)

- **Every stem anchors to lines.** "the repetition of negative diction in lines 21–27", "the sentence in lines 44–50 ('The daily ... conventionalities') reveals that", "the metaphor of 'chains' (lines 46 and 51) chiefly serves to", "in context, 'dallying in the shallows' (line 3) most nearly means". A mimic item without a line reference is not an AP item.
- **Stem families, in the proportions above:** function of a choice ("the main purpose of ... is to", "chiefly serves to emphasize"); the speaker's or narrator's attitude or perspective ("might best be described as that of", "reveals that the narrator"); meaning in context (a word or phrase, "most nearly means", "most probably refers to"); structure and relation between parts ("in relation to the second paragraph, the third paragraph", "in relation to the first sentence, the remainder of the passage serves to"); the whole ("the poem as a whole is best understood as a"); contrast pairs ("which best describes the difference in the way X and Y are presented": two-word pairs as choices); tone and effect of a sentence or image.
- **Choices are four, parallel in grammar, and the distractors are readings a careful student could hold:** the vivid surface instead of the attitude; the right content with the wrong function ("inject comedy" for "call the modernity into question"); an answer true of one part offered for the whole; the lay sense of a word for its sense in context. In a mimic, each wrong choice is named by its trap.
- **Answer-choice vocabulary is exam vocabulary:** vindication, deplores, candid, plodding exertion, measured patience, resignation. The Words rows in `SKILLS.md` exist for this.
- **Free response** uses stable wording: Q1 "In [poet]'s poem [title], published in [year], the speaker [what is addressed]. Read the poem carefully. Then, in a well-written essay, analyze how [poet] uses poetic elements and techniques to [convey/portray/develop a complex ...]"; Q2 the same with a 600–800-word prose passage and "literary elements and techniques"; Q3 a literary concept plus a list of about 40 works, "analyze how [the concept] contributes to an interpretation of the work as a whole". The rubric is the same six points for all three: thesis 1, evidence and commentary 4, sophistication 1.

## Sources on disk

Real items come first. When the learner adds one of these to this folder, list it in RESOURCES.md and use it from there:
- The College Board Course and Exam Description (CED) for this course, free on AP Central: its sample questions come with keys and a skill table.
- Released free-response questions with scoring guidelines (AP Central, past years).
- AP Classroom results the learner exports or pastes.
- The teacher's own worksheets, reviews and tests, which outrank everything else as the model for this class.

## Building a set

1. Prefer real items: an unused AP Classroom set, the CED samples, or a released passage.
2. Otherwise build a mimic with the `ap-lit-practice` skill: one passage (a public-domain poem of 100–400 words or a prose passage of 600–800), 8 to 13 four-choice items in the stem families above with line anchors, in exam order (local to whole), each with the key, the skill category, and the trap behind each distractor. Alternate poetry and prose fiction week to week; drama once a month.
3. Save to `practice/YYYY-MM-DD_<text>.md` (the skill's exam page alongside). The heading says **mimic, not released**.
4. After: score under exam timing, each miss argued against the text (why the distractor looked right, what defeats it), misses to the MCQ, Words and Text analysis rows in `SKILLS.md`, one log line below. Essays are built and graded by `writing/`, not here.
